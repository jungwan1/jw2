The same standing in a Korean court

Article 2 of the Debtor Rehabilitation and Bankruptcy Act (채무자 회생 및 파산에 관한 법률) gives a foreign national the same status as a Korean national for the purposes of that Act. A foreigner residing in Korea may apply to a Korean court for personal rehabilitation or personal bankruptcy on that basis. The court reviews income, assets, debts, and which court has jurisdiction. Living in Korea is not, by itself, a decision on the application.

Questions that come up often

What is personal rehabilitation?

A repayment plan is filed with the court and, if the court confirms it, payments follow that plan. A discharge of any balance is a separate court decision. This page does not state a ratio.

Does income have to be the same every month?

A salaried position is not the only pattern the procedure can look at. Repeated income from part-time work, daily work, or a business can be discussed. If there is little income, bankruptcy is considered as well. The court decides.

What is personal bankruptcy?

Where debts cannot be met even after assets are applied, a person may petition for bankruptcy and, separately, for a discharge. An adjudication and a discharge review are different steps.

Are company obligations treated like personal ones?

A procedure for a company’s own assets is different from personal rehabilitation. A personal responsibility for a company obligation is looked at on its own.

Does filing lift a seizure at once?

No. A stay or a prohibition order takes effect only after the court issues it. Wages, deposits, and real estate are not treated the same way.

Which documents come up often?

A resident registration certificate, income records, creditor records, and asset records. The list differs by case. This page does not collect the documents for you.

Can I request a consultation if I live outside Seoul?

The court is the one with jurisdiction over the registered address. A consultation request can be left from anywhere. The office is in Seocho, Seoul.

What do the fees look like?

Attorney fees differ by case, so no amount is listed here. Court stamp duty and service fees are paid separately and depend on the procedure and the number of creditors. This page does not promise payment in installments, or a refund.

Can a foreign resident apply in a Korean court?

Article 2 of the Debtor Rehabilitation and Bankruptcy Act gives a foreign national the same status as a Korean national under that Act. A foreigner residing in Korea may apply. Whether a case proceeds depends on the court’s review of income, assets, debts, and jurisdiction.
